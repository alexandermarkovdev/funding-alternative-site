Frequently Asked Questions

Introducer FAQs

See Our Full List Of FAQ

What is an introducer and can I refer clients to you?

An introducer is a broker who works for a brokerage and refers clients in need of business funding to us. If you regularly work with UK businesses that may benefit from short-term cash flow finance, we would be happy to work with you. There is no cost to partner with Funding Alternative.

How does the introducer relationship work?

It is simple. You submit a client’s details to us, we handle the assessment, offer, and funding process, and once a deal completes you receive a commission payment. We keep you informed throughout so you always know where your client’s case stands and we may ask you for further information along the way to help us with our review.

How do I submit an application for my client?

You have two options. You can submit applications through our introducer portal, which gives you a dedicated dashboard to track every case in real time and typically results in a faster turnaround.

Alternatively, you can email your client’s details directly to us at applications@fundingalternative.co.uk and we will handle it from there. Either way, we will keep you updated at every stage.

What does my brokerage need to do before I can start introducing clients?

Before any introducer can submit applications on behalf of a brokerage, we require a signed introducer agreement from a director of that brokerage. This is a straightforward one-time process that covers both parties and allows all introducers working under that brokerage to get started. If you are unsure who needs to sign or what the agreement covers, get in touch and we will walk you through it.

How and when do I get paid?

Commission is paid once a deal has completed, funds have been released to your client and they have met product specific repayment milestones. We process payments promptly and will confirm the expected timeline when your brokerage is set up with us. You will receive a clear breakdown of what you have earned for each case and when.
